2 · What adds and multiplies

◆
  • GDP = C + I + G + (X − M). Transfers never go in.
  • Real GDP = base-year prices × this year’s quantities. In the base year, real = nominal.
  • Solow: Y = A√K  ·  I = s × Y  ·  D = δ × K  ·  C = Y − I. Steady state: I = D.
  • Rate to count: LF = LFPR × population → U = rate × LF → E = LF − U.
  • Saving: S = Y − C − G  ·  private = Y − T − C  ·  public = T − G.
  • Open economy: I = S + (M − X).

3 · What you missed before

◆
  1. Transfers are not in GDP. Gondor = $6,200.
  2. An import cancels: I up, net exports down, GDP unchanged.
  3. In the base year, real GDP = nominal GDP ($56,100).
  4. The GDP identity is an accounting tautology — it proves no causation.
  5. Total sales are larger than GDP (they include intermediate goods).
  6. A firm buying machinery is I, not C.
  7. Solow, K = 80: $8.94, $0.89, $8.05, $0.80, add capital. With A = 1.1: K* = 121, Y* = $12.10, C* = $10.89.
  8. Long-run growth is not guaranteed.
  9. A less wasteful production method is innovative growth, not catch-up.
  10. A higher savings rate shifts only the investment curve.
  11. Gondor employed = 42,300,000. Check every digit.
  12. Natural rate = (0.5 + 0.5) ÷ 50 = 2%.
  13. CPI 75 to 100 = 33.33%. Divide by the earlier year.

4 · Ideas most likely to be asked

◆
  • Unemployed = no job, wants one, searched in the last 4 weeks. Stopped searching = not in the labor force.
  • Frictional = searching. Structural = skills do not fit. Cyclical = recession. Seasonal = time of year.
  • Catch-up growth = more inputs. Innovative growth = better ideas, and it lasts.
  • Only technology raises output forever. Saving raises the level only.
  • Disinflation = prices rise more slowly. Deflation = prices fall.
  • The CPI overstates inflation (quality, substitution, new goods).
  • GDP counts final goods, by location. Not home production, used goods, or stocks.
  • Fallacy of composition: true for one is not always true for all.
  • Institutions are permanent and broad; policies are narrow and easy to change.
  • Direct financing = straight to the borrower. Indirect = through a bank.
